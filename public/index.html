GREAT LAKES PHENOMENON

Why monarchs can “stack up” on the lakes

During fall migration, shorelines, peninsulas, islands, headwinds and passing fronts can concentrate monarchs. They may stream along shore, gather before a crossing, or form overnight roosts.

500+monarchs have been documented in individual Great Lakes roost observations.

The Great Lakes layer marks known context areas; it does not claim every point is an active roost today.

MIGRATION IN 60 SECONDS

The trip is stranger than it looks

The orange dots are only the visible surface of a remarkable annual cycle.

01

Northbound takes generations

Spring recolonization is a relay. The butterflies reaching the northern breeding range are descendants of the monarchs that left Mexico.

02

Southbound is the marathon

The fall migratory generation can live for many months and eastern monarchs may travel more than 2,000 miles to central Mexico.

03

The wave has a clock

Latitude and the seasonal sun create a repeatable timing pattern, while fronts, wind and temperature can speed up, stall or concentrate the flight.

04

There are two major migrations

Eastern monarchs generally winter in Mexico. Western monarchs primarily use coastal California and northern Baja, with some mixing between populations.

HOW THE ENGINE THINKS

One score, four ingredients

The score is deterministic and inspectable. If a source fails, the confidence falls instead of inventing precision.

40%

Recent observations

Licensed community-science records near the selected location and whether reporting is rising or falling.

30%

Historical timing

Published latitude-based arrival and fall peak phenology. Western locations receive less timing weight.

25%

Flight weather

Temperature, precipitation and fall southbound tailwind from the National Weather Service.

5%

Freshness

Stale or failed sources reduce the model instead of being silently treated as current.
